# Cat Breeds Knowledge Base

## Persian
Q: persian cat | persian | long hair cat
A: Persians are calm, affectionate cats with long coats needing daily grooming. Prefer quiet homes. Flat faces may need extra eye care. Medium size. Good indoor companions; less interested in high-energy play than some breeds.

## Siamese
Q: siamese | siamese cat
A: Siamese cats are vocal, social, and intelligent. Short coat, striking colour points, strong bond with people. Need interaction and enrichment. Usually do well with other social cats when introduced carefully.

## Maine Coon
Q: maine coon | maine coon cat | giant cat
A: Maine Coons are large, gentle "gentle giants." Semi-long coat needs regular brushing. Friendly with families and often other pets. Playful but not hyper. Check space and carrier size when arranging meetups via Pet Connect.

## British Shorthair
Q: british shorthair | british short hair | chubby grey cat
A: British Shorthairs are easygoing, sturdy cats with dense coats. Moderate activity, independent but affectionate. Classic blue-grey look is common. Good apartment cats; generally tolerant companions.

## Indian Domestic / Indie Cat
Q: indie cat | indian cat | desi cat | street cat | mongrel cat
A: Indie (domestic short/medium hair) cats are adaptable, clever, and common across India. Varied colours and temperaments. Often hardy when vaccinated and well cared for. Frequently listed on Pet Connect in cities.

## Bengal
Q: bengal cat | bengal
A: Bengals are athletic, high-energy cats with distinctive spotted/marbled coats. Need climbing space and interactive play. Not ideal for quiet, low-stimulation homes. Introduce carefully to calmer cats.

## Ragdoll
Q: ragdoll | ragdoll cat
A: Ragdolls are large, relaxed, people-oriented cats known for going limp when held. Semi-long coat, blue eyes. Prefer indoor living and gentle companions. Groom regularly to prevent mats.

## Sphynx
Q: sphynx | hairless cat
A: Sphynx cats are hairless and need regular skin care (baths, warmth). Extremely affectionate and attention-seeking. Sensitive to sun and cold. Unique look — discuss care needs before matching.

## Scottish Fold
Q: scottish fold | fold ear cat
A: Scottish Folds have folded ears and sweet temperaments. Important: cartilage/bone health concerns exist in the breed — responsible owners should discuss health openly. Soft, adaptable companions.

## American Shorthair
Q: american shorthair
A: American Shorthairs are sturdy, easygoing hunters historically. Low-maintenance coats, moderate energy, usually good with families. Reliable companion breed for first-time cat owners.

## Cat size guide
Q: small cats | large cat breeds | cat sizes
A: Larger: Maine Coon, Ragdoll. Medium: British Shorthair, Bengal, American Shorthair, Indie. Oriental/finer: Siamese. Size and energy matter for safe multi-cat homes.

## Indoor vs outdoor cats
Q: indoor cat | outdoor cat | keep cat inside
A: Many urban owners keep cats indoors for safety. Enrichment (trees, toys, window views) is essential. Pet Connect meetups should be supervised and gradual — never force introductions.

## Cat grooming
Q: cat grooming | shedding cats | long hair cat care
A: Longhair (Persian, Maine Coon, Ragdoll) need frequent brushing. Shorthairs shed seasonally. Hairless Sphynx need skin care. Mat prevention matters for comfort and skin health.

## Cat vaccination
Q: cat vaccines | feline vaccines | rabies cat
A: Typical cores include rabies and panleukopenia/calici/herpes combinations — follow your vet. Pet Connect vaccination status helps other owners. Not a substitute for veterinary care.

## Multi-cat compatibility
Q: can cats live together | introduce two cats | cat matching
A: Slow introductions, separate resources (litter, food, vertical space), and similar energy levels help. Use Pet Connect matches to coordinate careful meetups — don't rush same-day cohabitation.

## Kittens vs adults
Q: kitten breeding | young cat
A: Only healthy, mature cats should have breeding availability enabled. Kittens need vaccines and socialisation first. Age on Pet Connect comes from date of birth.

## Litter and care basics
Q: litter box | how many litter boxes | cat care basics
A: Rule of thumb: one litter box per cat plus one extra. Fresh water, scratching posts, and play reduce stress. For medical issues, see a vet — the assistant only covers general Pet Connect and breed info.
